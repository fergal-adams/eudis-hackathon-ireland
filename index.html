Autonomy on the battlefield

We're looking for innovative teams interested in participating in Ireland's first ever Defence Hackathon.

Teams will be competing for a €20k prize fund, with winners also getting selected for the EUDIS Mentoring Program. There are also a range of follow-on support services available to competing teams, including:

  • Free incubation space in DCU:Innovate Hub
  • Facilitation toward ESA BIC and other non-dilutive startup funding mechanisms
  • Test & validation opportunities with the Defence Forces Research Technology & Innovation unit
  • Post-hackathon AI compute credits from TensorX
  • Exposure to the Deloitte Technology Fast 50 programme

Why attend?

  • Be one of 100+ participants for Ireland's first ever Defence Hackathon, in partnership with industry, academia, the Irish Defence Forces and the wider Irish deep-tech innovation ecosystem. You'll be surrounded by mentors with deep domain expertise across military end users, startup advisory, venture capital, AI, spacetech, advanced drone technology and advanced manufacturing.
  • Teams will be competing for a €20,000 prize fund: 1st place €10,000 and selection for the EUDIS Mentoring Program (winner chosen by a jury of leading Irish defence-tech experts), 2nd place €6,000, 3rd place €4,000. Multiple teams can also avail of follow-on supports to keep developing their projects afterwards.
  • Curated and selected teams will be invited to a number of drone workshops in the weeks before the hackathon, to help advance their ideas ahead of the event.

Process

  • Applications are open now — deadline is 15 September 2026.
  • Be selected and attend physically at DCU:Innovate Hub, Glasnevin, Dublin.
  • Hackathon spans 15–17 October 2026 (2.5 days), with a wrap-up networking party at the end.
  • Pick one of the three challenges to work on, in a team of minimum 3, maximum 10 people.
  • As part of the selection process, we'll favour existing ideas, prototypes or MVPs (clearly stating what existed before and what was developed during the hackathon).

Who can apply?

  • 18+ years old
  • Citizens of the EU, Norway or Ukraine
  • Able to participate on-site for the full duration
  • Part of a team (minimum 3 members) before the competition begins

⚠️ Eligibility verification: identification documents will be checked on-site. Failure to provide valid proof of citizenship may result in disqualification.

Challenges — which one will you solve?

Theme: Autonomy on the Battlefield.

The 2026 EUDIS Hackathon focuses on autonomous systems and robotics to gain an operational advantage in the air, on land, at sea and in space. Learn more: eudis-hackathon.eu/challenges

1. Navigation without GNSS

Autonomous systems that can navigate and operate effectively without GNSS, using onboard sensors and real-time perception. Develop or use data from quantum sensors (e.g. accelerometers, gravimeters and quantum imaging), which offer advantages such as navigation without GPS, the detection of underground structures, and enabling non-line-of-sight perception.

2. Coordination of Autonomous Swarms

Develop a multi-agent drone or robot swarm that can collaborate to complete a mission (e.g. search and rescue, mapping or target tracking) with minimal human control and include optimisation using quantum simulation.

3. Department of Defence Challenge

Space-Enabled Maritime Domain Awareness. Develop an autonomous or AI-enabled capability that uses data from space and/or remote sensors to detect, monitor, interpret or predict activity in real time within the maritime environment. Solutions may focus on:

  • Protection of critical undersea infrastructure
  • Detection of suspicious maritime behaviour
  • Autonomous surveillance of large ocean areas
  • Space-enabled intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance
  • AI-assisted interpretation of satellite imagery
  • Maritime anomaly detection
  • Predictive risk modelling
  • Multi-domain data fusion
  • Decision support for commanders and operators

IP

Participants retain ownership of the intellectual property they develop during the hackathon.

If a solution is developed by a team, the team members are responsible for agreeing internally how any intellectual property rights are shared between them.

Teams may also choose to make their work open source or license it in another way, provided they have the right to do so and respect any third-party rights, tools, code, datasets or libraries used in their solution.

EUDIS

The EUDIS Defence Hackathon is an instrument enabled by the European Defence Fund (EDF) to strengthen defence innovation in the European Union. It aims to bring together the brightest minds across Europe to collaborate on key challenges related to defence, cybersecurity and resilience.

The hackathon takes place simultaneously in eight European countries — Austria, Cyprus, Ireland, Italy, Latvia, Norway, Portugal and Sweden.
